Let's check on your device

Device Rescue looks over your Mac, Windows PC, or Linux machine for signs of trouble: unwanted software, risky settings, and things worth double-checking. It explains what it finds in plain language, so you can decide what to do next.

Read-only by default. Device Rescue only looks. It never changes anything on your device without your explicit OK.

SPEC A

Your computer as a city

There's one district for each kind of problem. A critical finding is a dark tower and a warning is a dark block. Fog means a check couldn't run, and it says so: it's never shown as clear. Info findings go in each district's Notes, so they don't crowd the map. The List / City switch goes back to the normal results at any time.

A mission briefing: one real finding

The briefing is the finding's own description, plus steps from its remediation walkthrough. "Fix it for me" opens Device Rescue's existing confirmation, word for word (shown open here), so nothing happens without a yes. "This is mine" uses the existing mark flow. "I did it" is for doing the steps yourself. Every one of these ends in a re-check.

The Gatehouse / BACKGROUND ITEM

Investigate suspicious agent:
com.mediaupdate.helper

CRITICALMADE BY: UNKNOWN · NOT SIGNED~20 MIN
THREAT INTEL

The launch agent com.mediaupdate.helper points to a suspicious location: ~/Library/Application Support/.mu/helper. Programs that start themselves from hidden folders are a common way adware and malware stay on a Mac after a restart.

YOUR MOVE
1Remove high-risk background tasksFrom the walkthrough "Review and clean launchd persistence".
FIX IT FOR ME?

This will: Stop the background job 'com.mediaupdate.helper' and move its settings file to Device Rescue's quarantine folder. Nothing is deleted.

To undo later:
  • Open Device Rescue and choose Undo on this item
  • Or move the file from ~/Library/Application Support/Device Rescue/Quarantine back to ~/Library/LaunchAgents and log out and back in

SPEC C
