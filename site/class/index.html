A free checkup for your computer.

Reclaim City is an app for Mac and Windows. It looks over your computer and tells you, in plain words, what it found and what to do about it.

  • It only looks. It changes nothing unless you say yes to that specific change.
  • It never needs your password. Not for your email, your bank, or anything else. If something asks you to type one into it, that isn't Reclaim City.
  • Nothing leaves your computer. It doesn't upload your files or your results.
  • Free and open source. Every check is a file you can read on GitHub.

What it isn't: antivirus, or a fix for your online accounts. It checks this computer; your email and bank get fixed on their own websites. A clean checkup lowers the odds of a problem. It can't rule one out.

What brought you here?

  • 🔓 I think I’ve been hacked Strange logins, changed passwords, accounts acting on their own
  • 🧭 I want to learn how to run a scan What you’ll see, how long it takes, how to read it
  • 🚫 I have a device that’s clearly infected And I don’t want it on the network

Worried someone in your life might be watching your devices? Looking is safe, but removing monitoring software can alert whoever installed it. Read this first, and talk to the instructor privately if you like.

🔓 I think I’ve been hacked

  1. Download Reclaim City and open it (the first time needs one extra step).
  2. Click Get started, then choose I think I’ve been hacked. That checkup takes a minute or two.
  3. Read Needs attention now first. Each item says what it means and what to do. Any fix asks you first.
  4. Your accounts get fixed at your email and bank websites, not in the app. Do it from a device you trust (your phone, or another computer), in case this one is recording what you type. Email first: new password, turn on two-step sign-in, sign out every other session. Then your bank.

🧭 Learn how to run a scan

  1. Download it below, and get past the first-open warning.
  2. First screen: “Let’s check on your device”. Choose Get started for the plain view, or Play it as a game for the same checkup as a city to take back.
  3. Pick a worry. “What’s worrying you?” lists eight situations. One more question follows: is someone in your life watching your devices? Your answer only changes what the app shows you.
  4. Run checkup. A progress bar shows each check as it runs. Nothing changes. Your computer may ask for permission to read some folders; a side panel explains each pop-up.
    • A specific worry: about a minute.
    • “I’m not sure, check everything”: 5 to 15 minutes, a few hundred checks. Let it run.
  5. Read the results, top to bottom:
    Needs attention now
    critical: start here
    Worth a look
    warning: when you have a moment
    Good to know
    info: background; most scans have lots
    Checks that couldn’t run are listed at the bottom. “Couldn’t check” is not “clean”: that check looked at nothing.

For the technical folks: the command line

Underneath the app is a read-only CLI, rescue. Install it from source only. It is not on PyPI; a package there with this name is someone else’s. 

git clone https://github.com/lizTheDeveloper/multiverse-device-rescue.git
cd multiverse-device-rescue
uv venv && uv pip install .     # or: python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install .
.venv/bin/rescue scan --no-downloads           # full read-only checkup
.venv/bin/rescue profiles                      # the situation checkups
.venv/bin/rescue export --profile digital_security_reset --stdout

--no-downloads skips the AI tools check, which otherwise fetches HackMyAgent from npm once. Leave off --auto and --yes: both apply fixes. Using an AI assistant? Point it at AGENTS.md. Every flag: CLI reference.

🚫 A device that’s clearly infected

  1. Keep it off the network first. Turn off Wi-Fi (or turn on airplane mode) and unplug any network cable. Don’t join the class Wi-Fi.
  2. Don’t plug it in to anything: no USB drives, phones, or other computers. And don’t sign in to your accounts on it.
  3. Don’t wipe or reset it yet. What’s on it now helps work out what happened.
  4. Ask the instructor. They can bring Reclaim City to it on a USB Rescue Stick, so it never has to go online. That stick is the one thing to plug in, when they say so.

Opening it the first time

Your computer will warn you, because the app isn’t signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate yet. That warning is expected here. It only happens once.

On a Mac
  1. Open the .dmg you downloaded and drag Reclaim City into Applications.
  2. Open Reclaim City from Applications. A box says “Reclaim City” Not Opened. Click Done (not Move to Trash).
  3. Apple menu → System Settings → Privacy & Security. Scroll down to Security, find “Reclaim City was blocked…”, and click Open Anyway. The button stays there for about an hour.
  4. Confirm with your Mac login password (or Touch ID). If another box appears, click Open Anyway. That password box is your Mac asking, in its own window; Reclaim City never sees it.

Right-click → Open no longer skips this on macOS Sequoia and later. Use the System Settings steps.

On Windows
  1. If your browser says the file isn’t commonly downloaded, choose Keep.
  2. Open ReclaimCity-windows-x64.exe. If a blue box says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. It installs itself and opens. No administrator password needed.

No Run anyway button? Smart App Control or a work or school policy is blocking it. Don’t turn those off; ask the instructor.
