A virus scan recognised a file as software that watches a person: it can
record what's typed, what's on the screen, or where you go. Sometimes it's a
parental-control or workplace tool someone set up openly. Sometimes it was
installed to spy on someone. Either way, whoever set it up may get an alert,
or notice it stopped reporting, if it disappears.

## Step 1: Decide whether it's safe to remove

What decides it is who gets what it records. If only you see its reports,
you can go to Step 4.

If someone else gets them, or you're not sure, don't remove or quarantine it
yet, even if you agreed to it at the time, or it's there "for the kids" or
"for work" on your own computer. Removing it can alert them. Talk to an
advocate first, from a phone or computer the other person can't reach.

## Step 2: Keep a quiet record

On paper or on another device, write down the file's name and where it was
found (both are on this item), and today's date. Take a photo of the screen
with your phone. That record can matter later, for a protective order or a
police report, and removing the software erases most of it.

## Step 3: Make a plan before you change anything

An advocate can help you decide when removing it is safe, and what to do
about the accounts it may have seen. Change passwords from a different device
the other person has never had access to.

## Step 4: Quarantine it when it's safe

When removing it is safe for you, choose **Fix it for me** on this item.
Reclaim City moves the file into its quarantine folder, where it can't run.
Nothing is deleted, and **Undo** puts it back. On Windows, use **Windows
Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history** instead.
