The scan found things that may point to someone else being on your computer. If
you start cleaning up right away, the information that shows what happened goes
with it: what was running, what was set to start automatically, and when. That
record can matter later, for your own understanding, for an employer's security
team, or for a protective order or police report. A few minutes of note-taking
first keeps your options open.

## Step 1: Write down what you noticed

On paper or on another device (not this computer), write:

- What first made you worry, and the date and time you noticed it.
- Any account that behaved oddly, and any charge you didn't make.
- Anything you have already changed on this computer.

## Step 2: Take photos of the screen

Photograph any warning, strange message or this scan's findings on the screen.
Photos are strong evidence, and they stay on your phone, not on the computer
that may be watched.

## Step 3: Talk to someone before cleaning up

- If this is a work computer, stop and call whoever handles security there.
  Don't clean it yourself.
- If another person may be involved (an ex-partner, a family member, anyone who
  has had access to your devices), talk to a domestic violence advocate before
  you remove anything: US National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233, or text START to
  88788. Removing monitoring software can alert the person who installed it.
- For everyone else, go ahead with the other steps in your list when you are
  ready. Nothing here asks you to delete anything.
