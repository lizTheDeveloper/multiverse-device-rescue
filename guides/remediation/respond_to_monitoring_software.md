The scan found software that can watch or control this computer. Some of it
is sold for spying on a partner or family member; some is remote-support,
parental-control or workplace software with honest uses. Either way, the
person who installed it may get an alert, or notice it stopped reporting, if
it disappears. Going step by step keeps you safer than deleting it straight
away.

## Step 1: Decide whether it's safe to remove

If you installed it yourself and use it (for example, remote support you
set up, or a parental-control app on your child's computer), you can stop here.

Otherwise, assume the person who installed it may notice when it stops
working. If that person is a partner, ex or family member, talk to a domestic
violence advocate before you remove anything; an advocate can help you plan
for what happens next. In the US: National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233,
or text START to 88788. Use a phone or computer the other person can't reach.

Workplace monitoring is made for an employer recording activity on work
computers; parental controls are made for a parent watching a child's
messages, browsing and screen time. To tell which this is: if you're worried
someone is watching you, treat it as monitoring software someone put here
without asking: don't remove it yet, and write down what was found and when.
If this is a work computer and you aren't worried, it's most likely there for
work; your IT department can tell you. If this computer is a child's and a
parent set it up, it's most likely that.

## Step 2: Keep a record

On paper or on another device, write down the name the scan found, where it
was found, and today's date. Take a photo of the scan result with your phone.
That record can matter later, for a protective order or a police report, and
removing the software erases most of it.

## Step 3: Secure your accounts from a different device

Monitoring software can see what you type on this computer, including new
passwords. Using a phone or computer the other person has never had access to:

1. Change the password for your email first, then your Apple Account or
   Microsoft account, then banking and social media.
2. Turn on two-step sign-in for each of them.
3. Sign out of every other session (most services have "Sign out of all
   devices" in their security settings).

## Step 4: Remove it when it's safe

1. Use the program's own uninstaller if it has one. Deleting only the files you
   can see often leaves a background copy that puts it back.
2. Run the checkup again. These products often install a background service
   and a startup item as well; the new scan shows anything left behind.
3. On a Mac, also look in System Settings > General > Login Items & Extensions,
   and in System Settings > Privacy & Security under Accessibility, Screen
   Recording and Input Monitoring, and turn off anything you don't recognize.
4. On Windows, also look in Task Manager > Startup apps.

## Step 5: Consider a fresh start

The most reliable way to be sure monitoring software is gone is to back up
your documents and photos (not apps), erase the computer and reinstall the
operating system. Think about this if anything still feels wrong after
removal, or if you can't tell what else was changed.
