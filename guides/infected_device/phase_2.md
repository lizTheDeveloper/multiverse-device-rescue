## Step 1: Use a spare drive

Copy to a USB drive you're willing to erase later, not a backup drive you
rely on. If there's a backup from before the trouble started (Time Machine,
an external drive), leave it unplugged: it may be the cleanest copy you
have.

## Step 2: Copy documents and photos only

Safer to copy: photos, videos, music, PDFs, plain text, and your own
documents and spreadsheets.

Leave behind: programs and installers (`.app`, `.dmg`, `.pkg`, `.exe`,
`.msi`), scripts (`.sh`, `.command`, `.bat`, `.cmd`, `.ps1`, `.vbs`, `.js`),
shortcuts (`.lnk`), and anything you don't recognize. You can download
programs again later from their real websites.

Office files with macros (`.docm`, `.xlsm`) can carry malware. Copy them
only if you need them, and don't turn on macros when you open them.

## Step 3: Open the copies carefully

Before you open the copied files on another computer, make sure that
computer's own protection is on and up to date (Microsoft Defender on
Windows; macOS's built-in protection on a Mac). Don't open anything from
the copy that you didn't put there.
