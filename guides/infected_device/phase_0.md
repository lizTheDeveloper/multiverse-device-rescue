## Step 1: Keep it off the network before anything else

If it's off, leave its network cable unplugged before you turn it on. If
it's on, unplug the cable now. Malware that can't reach the internet can't
send your files out, take orders, or spread to your other devices.

## Step 2: Turn off its Wi-Fi

On a Mac: Apple menu > **System Settings** > **Wi-Fi**, and turn Wi-Fi off.

On Windows: **Settings** > **Network & internet** > **Airplane mode**, and
turn it on. (The network icon on the taskbar opens a quick panel with the
same Airplane mode button.)

Do this as soon as it starts, before it has time to join a network.

## Step 3: Forget the saved networks

So it doesn't rejoin by itself later. On a Mac: **System Settings** >
**Wi-Fi** > **Advanced**, then remove each known network. On Windows:
**Settings** > **Network & internet** > **Wi-Fi** > **Manage known
networks**, then **Forget** each one. If you can't get that far, Wi-Fi off
and the cable out is enough for now.

## Step 4: Don't plug in drives you care about

Malware can copy itself onto USB drives. Only plug in a Rescue Stick, or a
spare drive you're willing to erase afterwards. Never plug that spare drive
into a clean computer and open programs from it.

## Step 5: Don't sign in to anything on it

No email, no bank, no passwords typed on this computer, and no new accounts.
A keylogger records what you type. Your accounts get fixed from a different,
clean device at the end.
